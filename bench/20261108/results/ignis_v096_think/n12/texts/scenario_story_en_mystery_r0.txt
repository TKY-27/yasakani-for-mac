It had not been built for beauty. The main dome, once cream and proud, had weathered to a gray-blue shell, and the service buildings around it were low, practical, and always cold even when the mountain sun shone on them. Inside, the air smelled of dust, warm electronics, and tea from the tiny kitchen at the end of the east corridor. That night, the smell of tea was the only thing that seemed normal.

She was the calibration engineer for the instrument bay—the woman who checked that the telescopes saw what they were told they saw. She had been summoned by Dr. Halden, the site director, who was standing in the doorway of the main dome in his dressing gown, his hair wet from a recent shower and already beginning to fall out in new places.

“Power failed forty minutes ago,” he said. “Backup generators are dead. We’re on the old emergency battery, but it’s not enough to run the heating or the doors.”

“I can hear the silence,” Mira said.

It was not a silence without sound. The wind moved through the service pipes outside, and somewhere deep in the building the emergency battery ticked and hissed.
